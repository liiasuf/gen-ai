IELTS Academic: Writing Task 2

Writing Task 2 is the second and more heavily weighted part of the Academic Writing test, recommended to take about 40 minutes out of the total 60-minute Writing session. In Task 2, candidates are given a topic and asked to write an essay in response to a question, statement, or argument. The minimum word count for Task 2 is 250 words. As with Task 1, writing fewer words results in a penalty, and responses that are too short are unlikely to develop ideas sufficiently to score well.

Task 2 questions generally fall into several common categories, although a single question can combine more than one of these elements: opinion essays, which ask candidates to state and justify their own opinion on a statement (often phrased as "To what extent do you agree or disagree?"); discussion essays, which ask candidates to discuss both sides of an argument and may also ask for the candidate's own opinion; problem-solution essays, which ask candidates to describe problems related to a topic and suggest possible solutions; and two-part questions, which ask candidates to address two related questions within a single essay.

A well-organized Task 2 essay typically follows a four- or five-paragraph structure: an introduction that paraphrases the question and clearly states the candidate's position or the essay's overall approach; two or three body paragraphs, each developing one main idea with explanation, examples, and supporting details; and a conclusion that summarizes the main points and restates the candidate's overall position. Each body paragraph should generally focus on a single main idea, expressed in a clear topic sentence, and should be developed with sufficient explanation and relevant examples rather than simply listing related points without development.

Writing Task 2 is assessed against four criteria, the same broad categories used in Task 1 but with different specific expectations: Task Response (how fully and relevantly the essay addresses all parts of the task, and how well-developed and supported the candidate's position and ideas are), Coherence and Cohesion (how logically the essay is organized into paragraphs, and how effectively cohesive devices such as linking words and referencing are used), Lexical Resource (the range, accuracy, and appropriateness of vocabulary, including the ability to use less common vocabulary and paraphrase effectively), and Grammatical Range and Accuracy (the range of grammatical structures used and the accuracy of grammar and punctuation).

Because Task Response is one of the four criteria, simply writing grammatically correct sentences with good vocabulary is not enough — candidates must also ensure that their essay directly answers the question asked, addresses every part of a multi-part question, and presents a clear, well-supported position throughout. A common mistake is to write a generally fluent essay that drifts away from the specific question, or that addresses only one part of a two-part question while ignoring the other.

Time management within the 40 minutes for Task 2 is often broken down into roughly: a few minutes for planning (understanding the question, deciding on a position, and outlining the main points of each paragraph), around 30 minutes for writing, and a few minutes at the end for checking and correcting errors in grammar, spelling, and punctuation. Skipping the planning stage often leads to essays that lack a clear structure or that run out of relevant ideas partway through.

Because Lexical Resource and Grammatical Range and Accuracy are assessed in both Writing tasks — and similar criteria, under the names Lexical Resource and Grammatical Range and Accuracy, are also used to assess the Speaking test — building a wide vocabulary and a strong command of varied grammatical structures benefits a candidate's score across multiple sections of the IELTS test, not just Writing.
