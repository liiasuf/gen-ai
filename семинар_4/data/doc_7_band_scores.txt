IELTS Academic: Band Scores and Scoring

IELTS results are reported using the IELTS 9-band scale. Each of the four sections — Listening, Reading, Writing, and Speaking — receives its own band score, ranging from Band 1 (non-user) to Band 9 (expert user), with Band 0 reserved for candidates who did not attempt the test. Listening and Reading band scores can be whole bands or half bands (for example, 6.0 or 6.5), based on a raw score out of 40 converted using a published conversion table that may vary slightly between test versions. Writing and Speaking band scores are determined by certified examiners applying detailed band descriptors for each of the four assessment criteria in those sections, and are also reported in whole or half bands.

The Overall Band Score is calculated by taking the arithmetic mean (average) of the four section scores — Listening, Reading, Writing, and Speaking — and then rounding the result according to a specific rule. If the average ends in .25, it is rounded up to the next half band. If the average ends in .75, it is rounded up to the next whole band. For example, an average of 6.25 is reported as 6.5, and an average of 6.75 is reported as 7.0. Averages ending in .00 or .50 are reported as they are, without rounding.

Band scores correspond to broad descriptions of English proficiency. Band 9 indicates an expert user, with full operational command of the language. Band 8 indicates a very good user, with fully operational command with only occasional unsystematic inaccuracies. Band 7 indicates a good user, with operational command though with occasional inaccuracies and misunderstandings in some situations. Band 6 indicates a competent user, with generally effective command despite some inaccuracies and misunderstandings. Band 5 indicates a modest user, with partial command that copes with overall meaning in most situations though likely to make many mistakes. Bands below 5 indicate increasingly limited ability to communicate in English.

Universities and other institutions set their own minimum score requirements, and these vary widely depending on the level and type of program. Many undergraduate programs at English-medium universities require an Overall Band Score of around 6.0 to 6.5, while many postgraduate programs, particularly in fields requiring strong written or spoken communication (such as law, journalism, or education), may require 7.0 or higher. In addition to an overall minimum, institutions frequently set minimum scores for individual sections — for example, requiring no section score below 5.5 even if the overall average meets the 6.5 threshold — to ensure that a candidate does not have a serious weakness in any one skill that could affect their ability to study successfully.

Because the Overall Band Score is simply an average, it is possible for two candidates with the same overall score to have quite different skill profiles. For instance, one candidate might score relatively higher in Listening and Speaking but lower in Writing and Reading, while another candidate shows the opposite pattern, yet both end up with the same overall average. This is precisely why many institutions look at both the overall score and the individual section scores when evaluating applicants — a candidate planning to undertake substantial academic writing, such as a thesis or dissertation, may need a strong Writing score specifically, regardless of their overall average.

IELTS results do not have an official "pass" or "fail" — instead, each receiving organization decides what score it requires for its own purposes. The same Overall Band Score might be sufficient for one university program but insufficient for another, more competitive program, or for a professional registration body with stricter language requirements. Candidates are encouraged to check the specific requirements of the institution or organization they are applying to well before booking their test, since requirements can change and may include conditions beyond the test score itself, such as the test's validity period (commonly accepted as two years from the test date).
