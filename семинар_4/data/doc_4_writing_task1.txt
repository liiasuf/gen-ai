IELTS Academic: Writing Task 1

The Academic Writing test lasts 60 minutes and consists of two tasks: Task 1 and Task 2. Task 1 is recommended to take about 20 minutes, while Task 2 is recommended to take about 40 minutes, reflecting the fact that Task 2 carries more weight in the overall Writing score (it contributes roughly twice as much as Task 1 to the final band).

In Academic Writing Task 1, candidates are presented with one or more pieces of visual information and asked to summarize, describe, or explain the information in their own words. The visual information may take several forms: a line graph showing change over time, a bar chart comparing categories, a pie chart showing proportions, a table presenting numerical data, a process diagram showing the stages of a process or how something works, or a map showing how a place has changed over time or comparing two locations. Sometimes Task 1 presents two or more of these visual elements together, requiring candidates to compare and synthesize information from multiple sources.

The minimum word count for Task 1 is 150 words. Writing fewer words results in a penalty, while writing significantly more than necessary can use up time better spent on Task 2 and may reduce the clarity and focus of the response. There is no strict maximum, but most successful responses fall in the range of 150 to 200 words.

A strong Task 1 response typically follows a clear structure: an introduction that paraphrases the task prompt in the candidate's own words, an overview that summarizes the two or three most important overall trends or features without going into specific numbers, and one or two body paragraphs that describe specific details and data, making comparisons where relevant. The overview is particularly important — many candidates lose marks by jumping straight into details without first stepping back to identify the "big picture" of what the graph, chart, or diagram shows.

For process diagrams, candidates should describe each stage of the process in a logical sequence, using appropriate sequencing language such as "first," "next," "after this," "following this stage," and "finally." For maps, candidates should focus on the changes between the two time periods shown (for example, what has been built, removed, or changed) or on the differences between two locations, using directional and spatial vocabulary such as "to the north of," "adjacent to," and "in the center of."

Task 1 is assessed against four criteria: Task Achievement (how well the response covers the requirements of the task and presents an accurate overview), Coherence and Cohesion (how well the response is organized and how effectively ideas are linked), Lexical Resource (the range and accuracy of vocabulary used, including vocabulary for describing trends and data), and Grammatical Range and Accuracy (the range and correct use of grammatical structures, including tenses appropriate to the data shown).

Common vocabulary for describing trends includes verbs such as "increase," "rise," "grow," "decrease," "decline," "fall," "fluctuate," "remain stable," and "level off," along with adverbs describing the rate or degree of change such as "sharply," "dramatically," "gradually," "slightly," and "significantly." Candidates are also expected to use accurate comparative and superlative structures when comparing data points, such as "significantly higher than," "the lowest proportion of," or "nearly twice as much as."

A common error in Task 1 responses is including personal opinions or speculation about why a trend occurred, which is not required and can actually be penalized under Task Achievement, since the task asks only for a factual description and summary of the visual information provided, not an explanation of its causes or an evaluation of whether the trend is good or bad.
